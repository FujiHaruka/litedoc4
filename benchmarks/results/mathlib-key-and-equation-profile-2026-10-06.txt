The key pass by component, and whether the reprinted set's equation cost is selection or amortization
=====================================================================================================
Date: 2026-10-06. Context: docs/multiversion/plan.md D7 (decided 2026-10-06: all versions from nothing, differential,
in one process) and U9 (the reader, the hybrid, print reuse). The two large items of a patched version are the key pass
(97 s on the M1) and reprinting (111 s for 17.5% of declarations) - benchmarks/results/mathlib-environment-patch-2026-10-05.txt
(the "patch log"), round R1. A design memo (not in the repository) proposed an incremental key pass with carried memos
("Option 1": 10-20 s on the M1, theoretical) and asked two experiments first: E1 (where the key pass's time is, and
whether it is in parts a memo can carry) and E2 (whether the reprinted 17.5% take 59% of the equation time because they
are the expensive declarations, or because the full run amortized shared work). The programs are the patch log's
(patchrun: the reader, the hybrid assembly, the patch, the seamed extractor, the print key G7 with the own record) with
probes added under CODE. A measurement in scratch Lake projects on this M1; nothing in src/ or extractor/ was changed.

QUESTIONS
---------
(E1) Profile `keysPar`, the key pass of the patch path, by component: records of the shown constants, name resolution,
     the declaration's own skeleton and hashes, assembly. Count calls per component, the distinct (namespace, constant)
     resolution pairs, how often the resolution memo resets at its 1,000,000 cap, the distribution of |S(d)| (constants
     shown per declaration), and how many alias / namespace entries change between v4.31.0 and v4.32.0. From the
     numbers: what can Option 1 reach (theoretical), and does its kill condition fire ("if the key pass is dominated by
     skeleton hashing, which no memo carries, Option 1's 10-20 s is wrong")?
(E2) One patch process, R0 v4.31.0 full, R1 v4.32.0 with reuse, profiled per declaration. For the declarations R1
     reprints that also exist in v4.31.0, compare their equation cost in R0 and in R1. Split equation time into
     generation and printing in a full run (earlier full runs recorded `eqGenUs 0`: why?).

HEADLINE
--------
(E1) The key pass is dominated by name resolution, not by skeleton hashing; the kill condition does not fire
    (measured, 1 thread, v4.32.0 keyed after v4.31.0, 566,246 candidates, 125.05 s of keyed time with the probe on):

    The resolution memo is not the problem: 1,811,891 distinct pairs (1,779,972 for v4.31.0); one reset in the 1-thread
    run and none in any thread of the 4-thread run (each thread ~720,000 misses, under the cap); 23,657 recomputations
    after the reset (~0.8 s). `getRevAliases`, the whole-state fold Option 1a proposed to memoise, costs 0.04 s.
    |S(d)|: mean 9.62, median 8, p90 17, p99 34, max 317 (RCLike); sum 5,444,675; |spaces(d)| mean 1.016.
    Between the versions: records 146,798 same, 22,321 changed, 4,182 new, 1,949 removed (of 173,301); resolution values
    1,759,318 same, 86 changed, 52,487 new, 20,568 removed (of 1,811,891); alias pairs in the environment 649 -> 640 (+9,
    -18), decoded alias entries 637 -> 633 (+14, -18); namespaces 26,198 -> 30,023 (+4,410, -585), which name resolution
    does not read (Lean v4.34.1's resolveGlobalName never calls isNamespace).
    Four threads do not scale (measured): per unit of work a thread costs 1.6-1.9x a lone thread for records and the
    declaration's own parts and 3.35x for resolution, on top of the duplicated work (records 1.94x, resolutions 1.58x).
    Marking the environment persistent (no reference counting; the environment then leaks) took the 4-thread pass from
    126.2 / 133.1 s to 74.7 s; 1 thread persistent 118.0 s. Reference counting on the shared environment accounts for
    R1's penalty (R0, non-persistent, was already 75.2 s); duplicated records do not. A per-unit cost of ~1.8x at 4
    threads survives persistence (74.7 s x 4 against 118.0 s x 1.38 of duplicated work), cause not measured.
    What Option 1 could reach (theoretical, from the 1-thread components; CPU, then 4-thread wall at the measured
    1.6-1.9x per-unit cost): 1a alone (shared memo, no carry) ~54-96 s wall, not the memo's 25-30 s; 1a+1b (carried
    memos, every declaration's own part recomputed) ~41 s CPU, ~16-19 s wall; 1a+1b+1c (only the declarations whose
    inputs changed: 161,006, 28.4%) ~23 s CPU, ~9-11 s wall. Both carried figures take the lower bound for resolution
    recomputation (the 52,573 pairs whose value is new or changed); if invalidation re-resolved every pair, they grow by
    up to 64 s of CPU. The 10-20 s of the memo is reachable, and what decides it is how tight the resolution
    invalidation is, not skeleton hashing.
(E2) Selection, not amortization (measured, within one process). In R0 alone, the 12,114 declarations with equation
    lemmas that R1 reprints are 21.7% of the 55,923 with lemmas and carry 54.9% of R0's equation time (246.7 of 449.3 s;
    59.9% of generation). Per declaration, the same 12,105 declarations cost the same in both rounds: generation 148.6 s
    in R0 and 151.2 s in R1 (x1.02), equations in total x1.14, while the control - their signature printing, the same
    work in both rounds - took x1.83 (the machine was slower in R1, see CONDITIONS). Lemma counts equal for 12,164 of
    12,169. The skew is a heavy tail: the reprinted set's 10 heaviest declarations carry 246.2 of its 442.0 s in R0;
    Mathlib.Tactic.* alone is 655 reprinted declarations with 298.7 s (reused ones there: 1,248 with 19.0 s).
    The generation / printing split (measured for the first time per declaration and on the patch path): of R0's 650.4 s
    of equation time, the 55,923 declarations with lemmas take 449.3 s - generation 248.1 s (55%), printer steps 191.2 s
    (43%), the rest 10.1 s - and 83 declarations without a lemma (79 counted equation failures) take 201.1 s, 31% of all
    equation time, which the probe does not split. 64 of those 83 are in R1's reprinted set and fail again there.
    Why full runs recorded `eqGenUs 0`: the generation clock exists only inside the pretty-print probe, which runs only
    with --pp-breakdown (--decl-profile switches it on); production runs pass neither. The probe is not broken: the
    levers log's profiled hybrid run of v4.31.0 recorded the split (eqGenUs 100,618,270 of eqUs 245,146,738) and it was
    never reported. What was missing was a per-declaration split; it is added (CODE).

E1 - WHAT WAS RUN
-----------------
One process, PATCH_STOP=keys, rounds v4.31.0 then v4.32.0. In each round `keysPar` ran several times over the same
environment ("variants"), the first unprobed, every later one checked against it:
  R0 v4.31.0: plain 4 threads, probe 1 thread, probe 4 threads, plain 4 threads
  R1 v4.32.0: plain 4, probe 4, probe 1, plain 4, then the environment marked persistent (Runtime.markPersistent,
              11.9 s), plain 4, plain 1
The probe (CODE: KStats / KProbe in PrintKey.lean) times each component with two clock reads and pins every timed pure
value (a pure `let` is otherwise sunk past the clock read; the extractor's `pin`). The 1-thread probe variant also keeps
every resolution value uncapped, the records, and one row per declaration (|S|, |spaces|, component times, own hash, SN,
SRES, G7, G7+own) and, in R1, flags each declaration whose shown records or resolution values differ from R0's.

OPTION 1, WHAT IT COULD REACH (theoretical; premises listed)
------------------------------------------------------------
Premises: R1's 1-thread probe components (v4.31.0 -> v4.32.0, non-persistent environment, M1); a record costs 46.6 us
and a resolution 35.7 us as measured; the per-declaration part (the keyOf time of a declaration minus the record and
resolution computations it happened to trigger) is 35.23 s over all declarations and 13.65 s over the union above; own
records 16.15 s over all, 6.15 s over the union; four threads give a speed-up of 4 / (1.6-1.9) = 2.1-2.5 when no work is
duplicated (the measured per-unit cost of records and own parts at 4 threads); invalidation bookkeeping costs nothing
(not measured).
- Today: 126-133 s (4 threads, non-persistent, this process), 97.1 s in the patch log's R1.
- 1a, shared memos with no cap, nothing carried: CPU 125.05 - 0.84 s of reset recomputation = ~124 s. Removing the
  duplicated work divides the 4-thread wall by ~1.38 (records x1.94 on 6.5%, own records x1.12 on 12.9%, resolutions
  x1.58 on 52.5% of the work): ~91-96 s non-persistent, ~54 s if the environment were persistent. The memo's 25-30 s
  for 1a assumed linear scaling, which the measurement contradicts.
- 1a + 1b, records and resolutions carried, every declaration's own part recomputed: 35.23 + 2.47 (own records at the
  record change rate, 15.3%) + 1.24 (26,503 records new or changed) + 1.88 (52,573 resolutions new or changed) =
  ~40.8 s CPU, ~16-19 s wall at 4 threads. Upper end if resolution invalidation re-resolved every pair: +63.7 s CPU.
- 1a + 1b + 1c, only the union recomputed: 13.65 + 6.15 + 1.24 + 1.88 = ~22.9 s CPU, ~9-11 s wall at 4 threads;
  ~23 s on one thread.
- The kill condition does not fire: the declaration's own skeleton is 15.1% of the pass (R1) and all own parts 16.0%.
  What Option 1 cannot carry without 1c is the per-declaration part as a whole (35.2 s, 28%: skeletons, lookups,
  aggregation, the rest). What decides whether 1b lands near its lower bound is the resolution invalidation: its probe
  set is an over-approximation of the 52,573 pairs that actually need recomputing, by an unmeasured factor.
- Contention is a lever of its own: the same pass on a persistent environment is 74.7 s instead of 126-133 s (4
  threads). The patch path needs a freeable environment, so this is not available as measured. It removes R1's
  penalty over R0, not the ~1.8x per-unit cost of 4 threads, which remains after it (cause not measured).

WHAT FAILED, AND WHY
--------------------
1. The first full E1 run took 14,564 s (4 h) and its times were thrown away. Moving the per-thread output arrays of
   keysPar into a loop that also carried the probe's rows made the arrays shared at every push, so every push copied the
   array: quadratic in the declarations per thread. 1-thread variants took up to 3,586 s, the unprobed ones 351-2,028 s.
   On the 20-module sample the copies cost 8 ms and were invisible; on the full data the time outside the probe's clocks
   (3,400 s of a 3,583 s task) showed it. Fixed by holding both arrays in references; a diagnostic run then gave 71.9 s
   (4 threads) and 108.3 s (1 thread) on v4.31.0 with the same key hash. The broken run also ran under heavy
   compression (mean 4.6 GB of the process compressed). Its counts (pairs, records, deltas) equal the rerun's.
2. The probe does not split the 201 s of the 83 no-lemma declarations: its generation clock is read only when
   getEqnsFor? returns, and these throw (inferred: the time is generation that fails). Not fixed in this run.
3. E2 ran under memory pressure the patch log did not have (see CONDITIONS); its absolute times are not usable as
   product figures, only its within-run ratios.

WHAT IS NOT MEASURED
--------------------
- The resolution invalidation's real recompute set (Option 1b): only its lower bound (52,573 pairs) and upper bound
  (all 1,811,891) are known. Building the probe-name index of the memo and counting is E4's work.
- The 1c union with records compared on the key's fields only.
