2. The idle pass, five heartbeats each
--------------------------------------
  `watch --interval 1000` on the tree above with nothing stale. The number is
  the loop's own ("N.NNN s to ask"), which times `Trigger::ask` and not the
  sleep. One heartbeat a minute; the run was 340 s.

    lean  2.235  2.253  2.263  2.243  2.251 s   -> 2.249 s, 19 passes/minute
    rust  0.063  0.062  0.066  0.065  0.057 s   -> 0.063 s, 56 passes/minute

  So a pass is 2.25 s in Lean against 0.063 s in Rust on this tree: **35.7x**.
  At the default --interval 1000 one pass takes 3.2 s in Lean and 1.06 s in
  Rust, so the core is busy 69% of the time instead of 6%.

  What is being hashed, from `ledger check --timings` on the same ledger:
    modules 422, files 422, hashedBytes 228,439,544
  which puts the Lean loop at 101 MB/s and the Rust loop at 3.6 GB/s. The whole
  of the gap is SHA-256 and none of it is the port: single-threaded Rust hashes
  the same 228 MB in 0.2877 s (794 MB/s, `concurrency 1` above), so ~4.5x of
  the 35.7 is Rust hashing on every core and ~7.9x is the SHA-256 itself. The
  decision not to take C for it was settled in M4
  (-> purelean-sha256-2026-08-31.txt).

  This does not fail anything -- tools/watch-gate.sh asserts on integers and on
  no duration at all. It is quoted in two places instead, and both were measured
  here rather than copied from the Rust source, which says 0.13 s:
  `src/Litedoc4/Watch.lean`'s module doc and its `--interval` floor refusal.
  **The floor stays at 100 ms**: `--interval` is a name tools/public-surface.txt
  promises, and moving what it accepts breaks a file somebody else maintains.

  Comparison with purelean-async-tcp-2026-08-31.txt §7, which measured 0.129 s
  and 2.033 s for the same question: that one timed a whole `litedoc4 ledger
  check` **process** over the 432-module ledger, this one times the loop's call
  over the 422-module tree the gate builds. Two denominators and two shapes; do
  not put the four numbers in one row.

3. Which of the three costs the investigation named actually bit
---------------------------------------------------------------
  Numbered as .claude/purelean-plan.md numbers them.

  cost 1, buffered stdout       BIT. Without a flush the gate hangs at the
                                banner for its full 60 s. Closed by replacing
                                the command's stdout with an `IO.FS.Stream`
                                whose `write`/`putStr` flush -- the lines a
                                reader waits on during a pass are `runBuild`'s,
                                not the loop's, and a helper the loop printed
                                through would not have reached them.
  cost 2, the poll              CONFIRMED and re-measured (section 2). Nothing
                                fails; two texts changed.
  cost 3, the port at `accept`  NOT OBSERVED as a failure, and the shape it
                                warns about is avoided by construction:
                                `Httpd.bind` issues the accept loop itself, so
                                nothing prints between `listen` and `accept`.

5. The two Step branches the gate cannot reach
----------------------------------------------
  tools/watch-gate.sh drives `idle` and `rebuild`. `settling` and `skip` need a
  world that moves between passes, so they were driven by hand on a copy of
  e2e/micro. One `ledger touch`, so that the loop has acted once -- the first
  rebuild of a session is fatal by design -- and then one source file with no
  olean (`Example/Ghost.lean`), which is the shape `skip` exists for: reported
  stale by every pass until `lake build` runs, and a rebuild that cannot
  succeed. Verbatim:

    watch   1 module(s) to re-extract - waiting one quiet interval in case
            something is still writing oleans                       settling
    watch   #1 the ledger reports 1 module(s) to re-extract          rebuild
    watch   #1 incremental - re-extracted 1 module(s), re-rendered 1 page(s),
            started Lean 1 time(s) in 0.321 s
    watch   #1 reload http://127.0.0.1:8488/
    watch   1 removed - waiting one quiet interval in case something is still
            writing oleans                                          settling
    watch   #2 the ledger reports 1 removed                          rebuild
    watch   #2 the rebuild stopped: --modules and the merged IR name different
            modules: 1 in the list with nothing behind them (Example.Ghost) ...
            (exit 3)
    watch   #2 not retrying until the oleans move again - a failing extraction
            restarted every 1000 ms would import 3 GB every 1000 ms
    watch   waiting - 1 removed, unchanged since the last pass acted on it, so
            there is nothing new to do (0.049 s to ask)                  skip

  The last line is the 60 s heartbeat: `skip` prints nothing per pass, so the
  heartbeat is the only positive evidence that the loop reached it rather than
  retrying. Between #2 and it, 80 s of polling produced no further line, which
  is the "not retrying" claim in the shape a reader can check.

  Nothing was written under e2e/micro: the copy was
  /private/tmp/lean-doc-relay/m6-steps, deleted afterwards.

Not measured
------------
  - Linux. Everything here is macOS/arm64, and ci.yml runs tools/watch-gate.sh
    on ubuntu-latest. libuv's deferred bind and SO_REUSEADDR are where the two
    are most likely to differ, and cost 2 above depends on exactly that.
  - The `settling` branch's five-second "still not quiet" report: reaching it
    needs an answer that keeps moving for more than five seconds, which means a
    real `lake build` in flight against the measurement target.
