of three Mathlib releases (each round decoded whole, merged by rewriteMerge as read-alone merges,
every print key computed afresh, the previous round's printed output reused where the key is
unchanged), against `reader extract` alone on the same versions, 3 alternating cycles

3 cycles, not 5: for lever experiments 3 alternating cycles are enough to tell an obvious win

**Verdict: E1 does not win on M; by the plan's kill rule ("killed if the peak stays above it")
it is killed.** It is exact and saves about 8-10 s per extra version, but the resident process
holds 1.7 GiB more than read-alone from its second round on. The prediction was rounds 2-3 at
30-33 s and peak about read-alone's (theoretical): time came in under it, memory missed it.

Reuse takes extraction from 17.6 s to 1.7-3.3 s; the fresh key pass gives 5.1 s of it back. The
session's decode is 0.7-1.0 s slower than read-alone's on the same version from round 2 on, and
clearing realizations costs 1.1 s from round 2 on against 0.07 s in round 1; neither is explained
by this run.

The round-to-round growth the patched session showed (mv-m-session-2026-10-10) does not appear
without the patch: decode 12.41-12.58 -> 12.68-12.75 s from round 2 to 3, against read-alone's
11.56-11.63 -> 11.71-11.76 s for the same two versions; finalize 0.35 s in both rounds. The
patched session in this same warm state (one cycle, no check flags) still grew: decode 11.94 ->
12.89 -> 14.74 s, hash 19.27 -> 20.22 -> 25.01 s; rounds 59.55 / 51.93 / 52.69 s, maximum RSS
5,035 MiB, every IR equal to read-alone (measured, one cycle).

The extra 1.7 GiB is not the patch's retained state: the patched session holds about the same
from round 2 on (resident 4,975-5,035 MiB before extraction). What holds it, live objects of an
earlier round or memory the allocator keeps after freeing, is not determined by this run.
