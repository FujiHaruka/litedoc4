purelean M5 — does the Lean incremental path start a Lean environment it does not need?
=======================================================================================
2026-08-31. The answer an incremental build gives most often is "nothing is
stale". The Rust half starts its resident extractor at the **first extraction
request** rather than at the start of the run, so that answer costs no import;
the Lean half had only a full path and always started one. This is the
measurement that says the port carried the laziness, rather than the argument
that it has a guard.

Conditions
----------
  machine     MacBookAir10,1 (Apple M1), 16 GB, Darwin 25.6.0
  Lean        leanprover/lean4:v4.31.0, `lean_exe litedoc4` built from `src/`
              through `cd e2e/consumer && lake build litedoc4/litedoc4`
  target      /Users/haruka/dev/lean-projects at 16ff7a40, 422 modules in the
              IR, 432 in the frozen module list (-> target-drift-2026-08-31.txt)
  extractor   extractor/build/extract, behind a wrapper that appends its own pid
              to a log and then `exec`s the real one
  command     litedoc4 incremental --serve --extractor-bin <wrapper>
                --target <target> --jobs 4 --mode self  (plus --ir --pages
                --ledger --work --state --modules --source-url --link-index)
  fixtures    the two scenarios `tools/incremental-reference.sh` builds:
              `nochange` (a re-run with nothing invalidated) and `self-one`
              (one module invalidated with `ledger touch`, the honest fake —
              the target itself is never written to)

Results
-------
  scenario     extractor processes started   extractSeconds   totalSeconds
  nochange                    0                    0.000            5.7
  self-one                    1                   14.9–17.4         23.4

The process count is the answer; the durations are shown because they are the
consequence and because they rule out the reading "it started one and was fast".
`self-one`'s extraction alone is longer than the whole of `nochange`, and
`nochange`'s 5.7 s is accounted for by detect (2.3 s, hashing 432 oleans),
global (1.4 s), impact (0.4 s) and render (1.5 s) with no room for an import of
6,011 modules.

Both runs report `"serve": true` in their timings record, and **that is not the
same claim**: it says the resident path was selected, not that a process exists.
Reading it as the latter is what makes a lazy start look untested.

Corroboration from the recordings
---------------------------------
`tools/incremental-reference.sh --extractor resident` over all seven scenarios:
three of them (`nochange`, `renderall`, `removed-one`) leave no extraction round
at all, and those are exactly the three whose round loop never asks for a module.

What would falsify the conclusion: a run that finds nothing stale and still
pays an import — visible as a non-zero process count here, and as a `nochange`
whose total approaches `self-one`'s.
