purelean M5 — what `ledger check` costs in Lean, and where the cost is
======================================================================
2026-08-31. `ledger check` is the answer an incremental build asks for most
often ("nothing is stale"), so what it costs is what the common path costs.
The Lean port hashes sequentially where Rust uses a thread pool, and the two
halves' SHA-256 are different implementations, so the question this answers is
not "which is faster" but **how much of the difference is the port and how much
is the hash**.

The control is `--algorithm lake`, which reads Lake's `<file>.hash` beside every
olean instead of the olean's bytes: 6.9 KB read instead of 227 MB, over the same
module list, through the same reader, the same key diff and the same comparison.
Whatever separates the two binaries there is the port; whatever appears only
under `sha256` is the hash.

Conditions
----------
  machine     MacBookAir10,1 (Apple M1), 16 GB, Darwin 25.6.0
  Lean        leanprover/lean4:v4.31.0, `lean_exe litedoc4` built from `src/`
              through `cd e2e/consumer && lake build litedoc4/litedoc4`
  Rust        target/release/litedoc4, the `sha2` crate with `asm` (the ARMv8
              SHA-256 instructions)
  target      /Users/haruka/dev/lean-projects at 16ff7a40
  modules     432 — `benchmarks/results/it-modules.txt`, which is frozen at 432
              while the sources hold 422; ten `Shannon.BroadcastChannel.*`
              modules' stale oleans are what still answer for the other ten
              (-> target-drift-2026-08-31.txt). 237,893,840 B of olean.
  concurrency 1 on both sides (the default), so this is not a thread-pool
              comparison
  warmth      warm — five consecutive runs each, converged; wall clock = user+sys
              from `/usr/bin/time -l` on every run but the first
  command     litedoc4 ledger check --ledger <ledger> --modules <list>
                                    --ir <ir> --source-url <url> --timings <f>
              with each binary reading the ledger its own `build` wrote

Results (`hashSeconds` from the timings record; run 1 is cold and shown apart)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  --algorithm sha256   (227 MB read and hashed)
    rust   0.3237 | 0.1357 0.1344 0.1346 0.1347   -> warm 0.135 s   RSS 9 MB
    lean   2.1167 | 2.1152 2.1142 2.1158 2.1289   -> warm 2.115 s   RSS 9 MB
                                                     ratio 15.7x

  --algorithm lake     (6.9 KB read, no olean bytes)
    rust   0.0548 | 0.0093 0.0092 0.0094 0.0095   -> warm 0.0093 s  RSS 4 MB
    lean   0.0120 | 0.0119 0.0121 0.0120 0.0120   -> warm 0.0120 s  RSS 6 MB
                                                     ratio 1.3x

What it says
------------
**The gap is the hash, not the port.** With the bytes taken out, the whole of
`check` — reading a 142 KB ledger, building both keys, diffing them, walking 432
modules, writing three files and a timings record — is 2.7 ms slower in Lean.
Under `sha256` the same run is 1.98 s slower, and 227 MB / 2.115 s is the
112 MB/s already measured for `Litedoc4.Sha256`
(-> purelean-sha256-2026-08-31.txt, where the rule agreed before measuring was
"Lean unless the measurement target takes more than 60 s", and the C route was
declined for its attribution obligation rather than for its speed).

So the two things that would move this number are the ones that were already
weighed: a thread pool (bounded by the core count), or C. Neither is licensed by
2.1 s against a 60 s budget, and **the Lean run's cost is CPU, not I/O** — user+sys
equals wall clock on every warm run, and RSS is 9 MB against 227 MB of input,
so the oleans are streamed and the page cache is not the variable here.

What would falsify the conclusion: a workload that hashes more than once per
build, or a target an order of magnitude larger.
